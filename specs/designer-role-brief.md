# Gamma — UX/UI Designer: Role & Time Commitment

**Status:** early-stage, pre-revenue, bootstrapped by a small founding team (Vik + 3 minority partners). A real product with real users today (2, scaling to 5 pilot testers soon) — not a concept pitch.

## The role, in one line

Part-time/freelance UX/UI support for Gamma's next few build phases — not a full-time hire, and not an open-ended retainer to start.

## Time commitment

- **Suggested: 5–8 hours/week**, structured around picking up specific design tickets from the Linear board rather than fixed daily hours. This is an async, ticket-driven team, not a 9-to-5.
- **Initial engagement: a 4–6 week trial**, covering the Coach Dashboard work below, with an explicit check-in at the end to decide whether to continue, adjust scope, or part ways. Neither side commits beyond that up front.
- No standing meetings required. A short weekly async check-in (Linear comment or a quick call) is enough to stay aligned.

## How the work is structured

Everything is tracked in Linear, not email or Slack threads:

- Design tickets live in the `Gamma` project, same board as engineering. Each links back to a spec in the repo's `specs/` folder (e.g. `specs/phase-2.5-coach-dashboard.md`) — **read the linked spec before starting**, the same rule engineering follows. A ticket description is a summary, not the full context.
- If a design decision changes what a ticket actually describes, that needs to land back in the ticket — and in the spec too, if it's a real scope or architecture change. Not something that only lives in a Figma comment.
- Design files (Figma or otherwise) get linked as attachments on the relevant Linear issue, so anyone on the team — including a future engineering session — can find them without asking around.

## What's expected

- Pick up tickets tagged for design from the milestones above; deliver work scoped to what the linked spec actually asks for, not a full redesign of adjacent screens unless the ticket says so.
- Flag it early if a ticket's scope looks bigger than it reads — better to renegotiate than silently absorb the extra hours.
- Hand off in a form engineering can actually build from — specs, redlines, or a walkthrough, whatever's lightest-weight and still unambiguous.

## What's *not* expected

- No full-time availability, no fixed daily hours, no synchronous/in-person requirement.
- No coding — implementation is Vik's (and engineering sessions') job.
- No obligation to touch what already works — the live screens are functional and in real daily use; change them only where a ticket actually asks for it.
